CHU Modular

A typeface cast by taking dots snapped to a grid and turning them straight into outlines, without tracing. In a single file, the weight changes seamlessly from 100 to 900.

MODULAR
Weight 400 — Regular
↑ Drag the slider and the samples below thicken with it
あいうえお カタカナ
Kana move on the same single axis too (from dots standing apart → to a filled surface)

Where it
comes from

This typeface was not drawn by hand. It came out of a tool called 鋳 CHU. Letters are snapped to a grid, dots larger than the cells are placed on it, and where they spill over and overlap becomes the outline. Nothing is traced, so each dot’s position and radius become the points as they are, and no letter drifts from the rest.

The weight axis is the size of those dots. Small dots stand apart as a row of points; larger ones fill the surface. The slider says “weight”, but what actually moves is the dots.

The tool is public. Change how the grid is cut, the shape of the dots or the width of the letters, and a different typeface comes out.

— Type / Four releases  ·  02 UWASA

UWASA

A typeface cast by taking the outlines of an existing typeface and roughening, chipping and scattering their edges. The breakup is applied to every letter by the same rules — which is why it is broken, yet still a typeface.

UWASA
うわさは かたちを変える
↑ Type, and the sample above becomes your text (kana go to the sample below)

Where it
comes from

A rumour changes shape each time it passes from mouth to mouth. Someone adds a little; someone drops a little. UWASA turns that “changing with every telling” into a typeface.

How it is made lives inside a tool called 噂 UWASA. It takes the outlines of an existing typeface and applies brush roughness, chips, fuzz and spatter to every letter by the same rules. Nothing is decided letter by letter by hand, so even broken, it reads as one when set.

⭐ Turn the knobs inside the tool and you can cast and download a rougher or a quieter UWASA on the spot. What is here is just one of them.

— Type / Four releases  ·  03 HON

HON

A typeface that holds each letter as a few strokes (a skeleton) and is cast by running a brush along them. Strokes taper at entry and exit, and traced marks overlap — a Latin face written from the bones, needing no source typeface.

HON
↑ Type, and the sample above becomes your text (capitals, figures and . , ' - only)

Where it
comes from

An ordinary typeface holds “outlines”; HON holds “bones”. Three strokes for A, three for H — only the strokes in writing order sit in a 0–1 box, and a brush runs over them to make the outline.

So strokes thin at entry and exit, corners sharpen and crossings overshoot. And when the same bones are traced once more, two strokes land slightly apart and overlap — the trace of writing fast, then going back over what was missing.

⭐ No source typeface is used, so this typeface belongs entirely to Kinoshita Studio (it is not derived from anyone else’s font). Turn the brush and hand knobs in the tool (奔 HON) to cast and download a different HON on the spot.

— Type / Four releases  ·  04 KETA

KETA

A typeface made by turning letters into a coarse grid picture, then moving each column’s width and each row’s height one by one. The picture stays the same, yet as the grid shifts the letterforms change — narrowed columns become stripes.

KETA
ますめのはばをうごかす
↑ Type, and the sample above becomes your text (kana go to the sample below)

Where it
comes from

Burn a letter into a 12×12 grid and keep only the black cells. Up to here it is a coarse dot picture, but KETA then changes each column’s width and each row’s height one by one.

The picture (which cells are black) never changes, yet moving the grid changes the letterforms. Narrowed columns become stripes; round the corners and it becomes a modular typeface.

⭐ Column widths and row heights are the same for every letter, so whatever you type, the grid lines up vertically. Pinch the grid in the tool (桁 KETA) to cast a different KETA.
